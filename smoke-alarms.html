Interconnected

Smoke in one room. Every alarm sounds.

Interconnected alarms give everyone in the home more time to get out. When one alarm detects smoke, they all sound together.

What the law requires

The Queensland rules.

From the Queensland Fire Department's fact sheet (May 2026).

  • Photoelectric alarms that meet Australian Standard AS 3786-2014
  • Interconnected: when one alarm sounds, they all sound
  • In every bedroom, in every hallway or room connecting bedrooms, and on every storey
  • Powered by the main power supply (hardwired) or a non-removable 10-year battery
  • Replace any alarm that contains an ionisation sensor, isn't interconnected, or is more than 10 years old
  • Replacing hardwired alarms? The new ones must also be hardwired, installed by a licensed electrician
Who it applies to

Every home by 1 January 2027

All homes, townhouses, units and manufactured homes. Homes that were sold or leased from 1 January 2022, and new builds and substantial renovations from 2017, already have to comply.

Questions

Smoke alarms FAQs.

Something we haven't covered? Call or text 0422 176 623.

Does this apply to my home?
Yes. Every home in Queensland must comply by 1 January 2027.
Do I need a smoke alarm certificate?
Yes, you’ll want one as proof your home meets the Queensland rules, and agents, landlords and buyers ask for it. Every install we do comes with a smoke alarm compliance certificate.
How do I check my current alarms?
Look for AS 3786-2014 on the alarm body, check it's photoelectric, interconnected and under 10 years old. Our free checklist walks you through it.
I'm a landlord or selling. Is it different?
Rentals and homes sold since 2022 already need compliant alarms. We can check yours.
How do I look after them?
Test monthly, vacuum away dust and insects, and never paint over or cover them.
